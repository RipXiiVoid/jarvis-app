Privacy policy

Whitehole, a voice assistant made by Vyan Raina. Last updated 10 November 2026.

Whitehole is built to keep as much as possible on your phone. This page explains what it uses, what leaves your phone, and why.

Listening for “Hey Whitehole”

When the wake word is on, your phone's microphone is checked on the phone itself for the wake word. That sound is not recorded, saved or sent anywhere, and is forgotten straight away. Android shows a small “Listening…” notification while this is on, and you can turn it off at any time in Settings › Wake word.

What you ask

After the wake word (or when you tap to talk), what you say is turned into text, on your phone or, for better accuracy when you're online, by Groq's speech recognition (the short recording is sent to Groq for that and not kept by Whitehole). To answer, the text and a short recent conversation history are sent to an AI service: Groq, OpenRouter or Pollinations, depending on the model you choose. Those services process it to write the reply. Whitehole doesn't sell or share it with anyone else, and doesn't use it for advertising. With Private mode on, nothing is sent: everything stays on your phone.

When you ask for a picture, your search words go to Wikimedia Commons, Openverse or Pollinations. Weather uses the place you ask about (or your rough location) with Open-Meteo, and news comes from BBC News' public feed.

Things on your phone

  • Contacts: read on your phone to find who to call or message. Never uploaded.
  • Calendar: read on your phone to tell you what's on. Never uploaded.
  • Location (approximate): used for weather and “where am I”, only when you ask.
  • Camera: only when you ask Whitehole to look at something. Photos are recognised on your phone; only a short text description of what's in them goes to the AI to answer you.
  • Messages and calls: Whitehole writes a message or starts a call only after you confirm it.
  • Memory: things you ask it to remember are saved on your phone only. You can see and delete them in Settings › Memory.

What's stored, and where

Your settings, chats and memory are stored on your phone. Whitehole has no accounts and no servers of its own, and it doesn't collect analytics or crash reports. Deleting the app deletes all of it.

Children

Whitehole isn't aimed at children under 13.

Changes and contact
